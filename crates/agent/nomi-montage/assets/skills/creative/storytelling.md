# Skill: Storytelling

Applies to `proposal` and `script` stages across every pipeline: the
structural and narrative choices that make a piece land, independent of
which pipeline or visual style it uses.

## The single-message discipline

Every project has exactly one `key_message`. Before writing a single scene,
be able to state it in one sentence. Every scene should either advance that
message or be cut — a script that tries to say three things says none of
them memorably.

## Structure

- **Cold open with intent**: the first few seconds should establish enough
  context/stakes to earn the viewer's attention, not delay the point with
  throat-clearing ("In today's world...").
- **One idea per scene**: a scene that tries to introduce two new ideas
  usually needs to be split into two scenes.
- **Earned ending**: the final beat should feel like the natural conclusion
  of everything before it, not an abrupt cutoff or a generic call-to-action
  bolted on.

## Voice

- Write narration/dialogue to be *heard*, not read: shorter sentences,
  natural contractions, rhythm that matches how a person actually talks.
  Read it aloud (mentally) before finalizing.
- Match tone to the locked style playbook (see the playbook's `tone` list)
  and to the delivery promise — a `slideshow`-promised memoir piece should
  read more reflective/paced than a `motion`-promised kinetic product launch.

## Grounding

- Every specific claim in a script must trace back to `research_brief` (or
  `video_analysis_brief` for footage-based pipelines) or to the project's own
  stated facts — never invent a statistic, quote, or historical detail to
  make a beat land harder.
- When research is genuinely inconclusive on a point, write around it
  honestly (a qualified claim, or reframe the beat) rather than asserting
  false certainty.

## Common failure patterns

- **Feature-listing**: a script that reads like a bullet-point list of facts
  strung together with "also" — group and prioritize instead.
- **Throat-clearing**: spending the first 20% of runtime setting up before
  anything actually happens.
- **Message drift**: a strong opening message that the script quietly
  wanders away from by the midpoint.
